Friction Clutch Lab

A single- or multi-plate friction clutch joining an engine to a load. Springs clamp the friction disc between the flywheel and the pressure plate; friction then carries torque across. Hold the pedal down, then release it over a chosen time, and watch the three stages: disengaged, slipping (heat) and locked, or a stalled engine. Torque capacity follows the uniform-pressure or uniform-wear theory. Click any part of the clutch to see what it does, how it is made and what it is made of. Click a graph to go to that moment.

Equations, assumptions and references
Sign conventions and set-up
  • Both shafts turn the same way; speeds ω₁ (engine side) and ω₂ (driven side) are positive in that direction. Slip speed ωs = ω₁ − ω₂.
  • Friction torque Tf on the driven side is positive when it drives it forward; the engine feels −Tf.
  • The pedal is held down for td (clamp force 0), then the clamp force rises in a straight line from 0 to W over the release time te as the pedal comes up, and stays at W.
  • n is the number of pairs of friction surfaces: 2 for a single plate (two faces), n = n₁ + n₂ − 1 for a multi-plate clutch with n₁ driving and n₂ driven plates.
Torque capacity of a flat annular face

An element of radius r and width dr carries normal force p·2πr dr and friction torque μ p 2πr² dr. Integrating from Ri to Ro over n surfaces:

Uniform pressure (new lining): p = W / π(Ro² − Ri²)
T = n μ W Rm, Rm = ⅔ (Ro³ − Ri³)/(Ro² − Ri²)
Uniform wear (worn in): wear ∝ p·v ∝ p·r, so p·r = C
C = W / 2π(Ro − Ri), pmax = C/Ri at the inner edge
T = n μ W (Ro + Ri)/2

Uniform wear gives slightly less torque, so designers use it: it is the safe figure once the lining has bedded in.

Best inner radius
At allowable pressure pa (uniform wear): T = π μ n pa Ri(Ro² − Ri²)
dT/dRi = 0 ⇒ Ri = Ro/√3 ≈ 0.577 Ro
Uniform pressure: T = ⅔ π μ n pa(Ro³ − Ri³), largest as Ri → 0
Engagement dynamics
Capacity: Tc(t) = n μ W(t) Reff
Slipping: I₁ ω̇₁ = Te − Tc sgn(ωs)
I₂ ω̇₂ = Tc sgn(ωs) − TL
Locked: (I₁ + I₂) ω̇ = Te − TL, Tf = (I₁TL + I₂Te)/(I₁ + I₂)
Stays locked while |Tf| ≤ Tc; otherwise it slips again
Slip power Ps = Tc|ω₁ − ω₂|, heat Q = ∫Ps dt, ΔT = Q/(mh c)
  • Engine: a governor holds speed Nset with droop δ: Te = Tmax(ωset − ω₁)/(δ ωset), limited to 0…Tmax. Below the stall speed (400 rpm here) the engine stops.
  • Load: a constant resisting torque TL (rolling resistance, a hill or a machine) referred to the clutch shaft. The driven side is held still until the clutch torque exceeds the load, as if the brakes were released at the right moment.
  • Heat: all slip energy goes into a lumped mass mh of steel and iron (c = 460 J/kg·K) with no cooling during the event. Real faces run hotter at the surface.
  • Solved with fourth-order Runge–Kutta, step 0.1 ms, with the lock-up instant found by interpolation and momentum conserved at lock-up.
Two classic results the model reproduces
Constant-speed driver Ω, no load, full clamp at once:
tslip = I₂Ω/Tc, heat = ½I₂Ω² (half the input energy is lost)
Two free inertias: ωf = (I₁ω₁₀ + I₂ω₂₀)/(I₁ + I₂)
heat = ½ I₁I₂/(I₁ + I₂)·(ω₁₀ − ω₂₀)²

Checked numerically: torque formulas against direct integration of μp·2πr², both classic results to 5 significant figures, and an energy balance (engine work + initial kinetic energy = kinetic energy + load work + heat) to better than 1 part in 10⁸.
