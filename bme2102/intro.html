1.1Theory of machines

Theory of machines studies the relative motion between the parts of a machine and the forces that act on them. It has four branches:

  • Kinematics: the relative motion of the parts, without asking what causes it.
  • Dynamics: the forces on machine parts in motion and their effects.
  • Kinetics: the inertia forces that come from the mass and motion of the parts together.
  • Statics: the forces on the parts at rest, with their mass taken as negligible.

Definitions

A machine transmits or modifies force to do useful work. A mechanism is an assembly of rigid bodies (links) that manages force and motion. A structure carries load without motion. Links meet at kinematic pairs; a series of links joined by pairs is a kinematic chain. A binary link joins two other links, a ternary link three. A lower pair has surface contact (a pin, a slider); a higher pair has point or line contact (a cam and follower, gear teeth).

The parts of a mechanism

Degrees of freedom

The degrees of freedom (DoF) of a mechanism are the number of independent inputs needed to fix its position. In a plane every free link has 3 (two translations and one rotation). Fixing one link and joining the rest with pairs removes freedoms: a lower pair removes 2, a higher pair removes 1. With N links, J1 lower pairs and J2 higher pairs:

Grubler (planar):   DoF = 3(N − 1) − 2J1 − J2
Kutzbach (spatial):   DoF = 6(N − 1) − 5J1 − 4J2
Both assume perfect joints and rigid links; real machines also have flexibility and friction.

1.2Kinematics and the choice of coordinates

Kinematics is the geometry of motion: it describes how bodies move without reference to the forces involved. It is used to design cams, gears and linkages, and to work out flight paths. A particle is a body so small compared with the curvature of its path that it can be treated as a point. If the particle is held to a path (a bead on a wire) its motion is constrained; with no guide it is unconstrained.

The position of the particle P can be given in rectangular coordinates x, y, z, cylindrical coordinates r, θ, z, spherical coordinates R, θ, φ, or along the tangent t and normal n to its path. Coordinates measured from fixed axes give absolute motion; from moving axes, relative motion. Most machine motions happen in, or can be drawn in, a single plane, so the rest of the chapter uses plane motion: z = 0 and φ = 0.

1.2.1Rectilinear motion

A particle moves along a straight line; its position is the distance s from a fixed point O. In a time Δt it moves a displacement Δs (negative if it moves the other way). Taking the limit as Δt → 0:

v = ds/dt = ṡ    a = dv/dt = d²s/dt² = s̈    and, eliminating dt,   v dv = a ds
s, v and a share one positive direction. A particle slowing down is decelerating; its a has the opposite sign to v.

Graphical integration

The slope of the s–t curve is v, and the slope of the v–t curve is a. Going the other way, the area under the v–t curve between t1 and t2 is the displacement s2 − s1, and the area under the a–t curve is the change in velocity v2 − v1. Choose a motion, play it, and drag the shaded window.

Position, velocity and acceleration against time

Plotted against position instead, the area under the a–s curve is a ds = v dv, so it equals ½(v2² − v1²). On a v–s curve drawn to equal scales, the normal at a point A cuts the s axis a distance CB beyond the foot of A, and CB = v dv/ds = a.

1.2.2Plane curvilinear motion

Now the particle moves along a curve in the x–y plane. Its position is the vector r from a fixed origin O. In Δt it moves from A to A′: the vector displacement Δr (a chord, independent of where O is) differs from the scalar distance Δs along the path. As Δt → 0 they agree, and

v = dr/dt = ṙ, tangent to the path, with magnitude v = ds/dt (the speed)    a = dv/dt = v̇
Rectangular components:   r = xi + yj,   v = ẋi + ẏj,   a = ẍi + ÿj
The acceleration is in general neither tangent nor normal to the path. The unit vectors i and j do not change, so their derivatives are zero.

1.2.3Projectile motion

Ignore air drag and the curvature and rotation of the earth, and keep the height change small so that g is constant. With x horizontal and y up, the accelerations are ax = 0 and ay = −g, and integrating gives:

vx = (vx)0    x = x0 + (vx)0 t
vy = (vy)0 − g t    y = y0 + (vy)0 t − ½ g t²    vy² = (vy)0² − 2g(y − y0)
g is taken as positive throughout. The horizontal and vertical motions are independent: one is uniform, the other uniformly accelerated.

1.3Kinetics

Kinetics brings in the forces and the masses. It rests on Newton's three laws:

  1. First law (inertia). Every body stays at rest or in uniform motion in a straight line unless an external force acts on it.
  2. Second law. The rate of change of momentum is proportional to the applied force and takes place in the direction of the force.
  3. Third law. To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Momentum and force

Momentum is mass × velocity. If a constant force changes the velocity of a mass m from u to v in a time t, the rate of change of momentum is (mv − mu)/t = m(v − u)/t = ma, so F = ma. A force is whatever produces, or tends to produce or destroy, motion.

A force changes momentum

Moment of a force and couples

The moment of a force about a point is its turning effect: the force times the perpendicular distance from the point to its line of action, F × l. Two equal, opposite, parallel forces with different lines of action form a couple; its moment is F × x, where x (the arm) is the distance between the lines. A couple has the same moment about every point.

Centripetal and centrifugal force

A mass m moving at speed v round a circle of radius r has an acceleration towards the centre, the centripetal acceleration ac = v²/r = ω²r. The force that produces it, the centripetal force Fc = m v²/r = m ω² r, acts inwards. By the third law the mass pulls outwards on whatever holds it with an equal force, the centrifugal force.

A mass whirled on a string

Cutting the string turns constrained motion into unconstrained motion: with no force left, the first law sends the mass off in a straight line along the tangent, at the speed it had.
