6.1What a cam does

A cam is a rotating part whose shape pushes another part, the follower, through a motion chosen in advance: up, hold, down, hold. Cam and follower touch along a line, so they form a higher pair. A spring, gravity or a groove keeps the follower on the cam. Cams open and close the valves of every four-stroke engine, and drive feeds in automatic lathes, textile and printing machines.

6.2Kinds of followers

Followers are named by the end that touches the cam, by how they move, and by where their line of motion lies.

Knife-edge follower

  • By the contact surface. Knife edge: simple but wears fast and pushes sideways on its guide. Roller: rolls instead of sliding, so wears far less; used in stationary engines and aircraft engines. Flat faced (mushroom): very little side thrust, compact, used for engine valves; offsetting it makes it spin and spread the wear. Spherical faced: a flat face rounded to cut the contact stress.
  • By the motion. Reciprocating (translating): slides in a guide. Oscillating (rotating): swings about a pivot.
  • By the path. Radial: its line of motion passes through the cam centre. Offset: it does not.

6.3Kinds of cams

In a radial (disc) cam the follower moves at right angles to the cam axis, as in everything above. In a cylindrical cam a groove is cut round a cylinder and the follower moves parallel to the axis.

Cylindrical cam with a reciprocating follower

the groove carries the follower: no spring needed

6.5Motion of the follower

Write S for the stroke, θO and θR for the cam angles (in radians) of the outstroke and return stroke, and ω for the cam speed. The time of the outstroke is tO = θO/ω. Pick a motion to see how its displacement diagram is constructed, and compare the velocity and acceleration of all four.

Constructing the displacement diagram

The four motions on the same outstroke

uniform velocitysimple harmonicuniform acceleration and retardationcycloidal
Uniform velocity: v = S ω/θO; acceleration infinite at the start and end of each stroke (unless the corners are rounded with parabolas).
Simple harmonic: vmax = π ω S/(2θO),   amax = π² ω² S/(2θO²)
Uniform acceleration and retardation: vmax = 2 ω S/θO,   a = 4 ω² S/θO² (constant, then the same retardation)
Cycloidal: x = S[θ/θO − (1/2π) sin(2πθ/θO)],   vmax = 2 ω S/θO,   amax = 2π ω² S/θO²
For the return stroke use θR in place of θO.

Only cycloidal motion starts and ends with zero acceleration, so it is the smoothest at high speed. Simple harmonic motion has the lowest peak acceleration but jumps in acceleration at the ends of each stroke; uniform velocity is acceptable only for slow cams.

6.6Drawing a cam profile

The profile is drawn by kinematic inversion: imagine the cam standing still and the follower turning round it in the opposite direction to the cam’s rotation. Lay off the outstroke, dwell and return angles from OA, divide them as in the displacement diagram, set off each displacement along its line, and join the points. For a roller follower the points form the pitch curve; the cam profile is the curve that touches all the roller circles. For a flat follower the profile touches all the follower faces. Choose Construct to build it step by step, Run to see the cam working.

Two things limit a design. The pressure angle (between the follower’s line of motion and the normal to the pitch curve) should stay below about 30° for a translating follower, or it jams in its guide: make the base circle bigger or the strokes longer in angle. And the cam must not be sharper than the follower: a roller larger than the smallest radius of curvature of the pitch curve, or a flat face on a cam that would need a hollow, cannot follow the motion (the tool warns you).
