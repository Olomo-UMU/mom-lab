Equations, sign conventions and references
Sign conventions
  • Crank centre O is the origin; the cylinder lies along +x. Crank angle θ is measured CCW from the inner dead centre (IDC/TDC, piston farthest from O). The crank turns CCW at mean speed ω.
  • Piston displacement s is measured from TDC, positive towards the crank. Piston forces are positive towards the crank (the direction gas pressure pushes on the power stroke).
  • Torque T on the crankshaft is positive CCW (driving). Rod obliquity φ: sin φ = sin θ / n, n = l/r.
  • 4-stroke cycle angle ψ runs 0–720° (0–180° suction, 180–360° compression, 360–540° expansion, 540–720° exhaust), θ = ψ mod 360°. The steam engine cycle is 360°.
Piston kinematics (exact)
S = √(n² − sin²θ)
s = r(1 − cos θ + n − S)
ds/dθ = r[sin θ + sin θ cos θ / S] = r sin(θ+φ)/cos φ
d²s/dθ² = r[cos θ + cos 2θ / S + sin²θ cos²θ / S³]
vp = ω ds/dθ ≈ ωr(sin θ + sin 2θ / 2n)
ap = ω² d²s/dθ² ≈ ω²r(cos θ + cos 2θ / n)
Gas pressure

4-stroke (ideal Otto): V = Vc + A s, Vc = Vs/(rc − 1). Suction at pin; compression p = pin(V₁/V)k; constant-volume combustion at TDC to pmax; expansion p = pmax(Vc/V)k; blow-down at BDC; exhaust at pex. Crankcase at 1.013 bar.

Double-acting steam: each side admits at ps up to cut-off β of its stroke, then expands hyperbolically, p = ps(β + c)/(x + c), with x the stroke fraction and c the clearance fraction. It exhausts at back pressure pb on the return stroke. Net force = (phead − pcrank)A (piston-rod area neglected).

Forces (two-mass, textbook)
mR = mp + mc·(lG/l)reciprocating mass
Fgas = (p − pback)·A
Fi = mR·apinertia force (opposes ap)
FP = Fgas − Fipiston effort
FQ = FP / cos φthrust in rod
FN = FP·tan φside thrust on wall
FT = FQ·sin(θ+φ)crank effort
FR = FQ·cos(θ+φ)radial force on crank
T = FT·r = FP·r·sin(θ+φ)/cos φ ≈ FP·r(sin θ + sin 2θ/2n)
Exact torque used for the engine

At constant ω, virtual work gives the gas torque, and the inertia torque is −d(KE)/dθ:

Tgas = Fgas·ds/dθ
Tpist = −mp ω² (d²s/dθ²)(ds/dθ)
Trod = −ω²[mc(xG′xG″ + yG′yG″) + IG φ′φ″]
IG = mc kG², φ′ = cos θ / S, φ″ = sin θ (1 − n²)/S³
Tcyl = Tgas + Tpist + Trod, T = Σ Tcyl(ψ − δi)

The rod's mass mc(1 − lG/l) placed at the crank pin rotates uniformly and gives no torque. The two-mass model is only statically equivalent, so the difference Trod − (−mc(lG/l)ω²s″s′) is the correction couple torque shown in the table.

Fluctuation of energy and flywheel
W = ∮ T dθ, Tmean = W / (cycle angle)
P = Tmean·ω
E(θ) = ∫₀^θ (T − Tmean) dθ
ΔE = Emax − Eminmaximum fluctuation of energy
CE = ΔE / Wcoefficient of fluctuation of energy
Ks = (ω₁ − ω₂)/ωcoefficient of fluctuation of speed
I = ΔE / (ω² Ks)required flywheel inertia

The speed trace uses ½Iω(θ)² = ½Iω₀² + E(θ), with ω₀ chosen so that (ωmax + ωmin)/2 equals the set speed. With that definition, the simulated Ks equals ΔE/(Iω²) exactly. Inertia torques are evaluated at the mean speed and the load torque is constant at Tmean.

Rim dimensions
v = πDN/60, σ = ρv²hoop stress in a thin rim
Dmax = (60/πN)·√(σallow/ρ)
mrim = I/R²rim supplies all the inertia
mrim = ρ·πD·b·t, b = (b/t)·t
Assumptions
  • Horizontal engine, so link weights do no work over the stroke; friction is neglected.
  • Idealised pressure cycles (instantaneous combustion, release and exhaust). Real indicator diagrams are rounded, so peak torque comes a little later and is lower.
  • Cylinders fire at equal intervals: 720°/N for 4-stroke. For double-acting steam the cranks are spaced 180°/N (N even) or 360°/N (N odd).
  • Mean speed and constant load are used for the energy method; the hub and spokes are ignored when sizing the rim.
