Equations, sign conventions and references
Layout and sign conventions
  • The pinion (gear 1) is on the left and drives anticlockwise. The gear (2) is on the right and turns clockwise; a rack is vertical and moves upwards.
  • P is the pitch point on the line of centres. The line of action passes through P at the operating pressure angle α′ and touches the base circles at the interference points M (pinion) and N (gear).
  • Distance along the line of action is u, measured from P, positive in the direction the contact point travels. Contact starts at K (u = −path of approach), where the gear tip meets the pinion flank, and ends at L (u = +path of recess), where the pinion tip leaves the gear flank.
  • The graphs follow one marked pair of teeth through the mesh. Pinion angle 0° is when that pair touches at P.

Assumptions
  • Rigid, accurate involute teeth with no profile shift. Load is shared equally between the pairs in contact; real teeth share it according to their stiffness, and tip relief changes the ends of contact.
  • When there is interference the drawing still shows the full involute. A real pinion cut by a rack or hob would be undercut below the base circle, which shortens the useful path of contact.
  • Coulomb friction with a constant coefficient μ. Rolling and windage losses are ignored.
