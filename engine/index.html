Four-Cylinder Engine Lab

An inline four-cylinder petrol engine, the kind cut open on the lab bench. Measure the bore, stroke, rod length and cylinder spacing on the real engine and type them in. The page works out where each piston is, how fast it moves and accelerates, the gas pressure in each cylinder, the torque on the crankshaft, how much the speed rises and falls with a given flywheel, and the shaking force and rocking couple that the engine mounts must carry. Drag the crank or click any graph to move the engine. The printable lab sheet goes with this page.

Equations, sign conventions, assumptions and references
Conventions
  • θ is the crank angle of cylinder 1, measured from its firing TDC in the direction of rotation; one cycle is 720°. Cylinder i has crank angle θ − δi, where δi is its throw position (0° or 180°).
  • x is the distance from the crank centre to the piston pin, positive towards the head. In the tables, piston speed, acceleration and forces are given as positive downwards (towards the crank), as in most textbooks.
  • r = S/2, n = l/r, ω = 2πN/60 (constant for the kinematics and forces; the speed graph then shows the small change the flywheel allows).
Slider-crank kinematics (exact)
sinφ = (r/l) sinθx = r cosθ + l cosφs = r + l − x
φ̇ = ω r cosθ / (l cosφ)ẋ = −rω sinθ − l φ̇ sinφ
φ̈ = [r(α cosθ − ω² sinθ) + l φ̇² sinφ] / (l cosφ)
ẍ = −rα sinθ − rω² cosθ − l φ̈ sinφ − l φ̇² cosφ

Two-term approximations: s ≈ r[(1 − cosθ) + (1 − cos2θ)/(4n)], a ≈ rω²(cosθ + cos2θ/n).

Cylinder pressure
V = Vc + A sVc = Vs/(rc − 1)A = πB²/4
dp/dθ = −γ (p/V) dV/dθ + (γ − 1)/V · dQ/dθγ = 1.32
Q(θ) = Qtot[1 − exp(−5((θ − θ0)/55°)³)](Wiebe)

Inlet valve closes 30° after BDC at manifold pressure pin; burning starts θs before TDC and lasts about 55°; exhaust opens 135° after TDC (blow-down), exhaust stroke at 1.08 bar. Qtot = 0.72 × (air trapped ÷ 14.7) × 44 MJ/kg, with 90% volumetric efficiency at 320 K. The 0.72 lumps wall heat loss and incomplete burning so that the peak pressure (about 50 bar) and IMEP (about 10 bar) match a typical naturally aspirated engine at full throttle (Heywood, 2018). The pressure curve is taken as independent of speed.

Forces and torque on one cylinder
mrec = mp + k mc(rod as two masses: k mc at the pin, the rest rotating with the crankpin)
Fg = (p − patm) AFi = −mrec aF = Fg + Fi
T = F r sin(θ + φ) / cosφ(equal to F·v/ω, by virtual work)
rod force F/cosφside thrust F tanφ

Rotating masses (crankpin and big end) are taken as balanced by the crank counterweights. The rod's correction couple (the two-mass model does not keep its moment of inertia exactly) is neglected.

Turning moment and flywheel
T(θ) = Σ TiTm = (1/720°) ∫ T dθP = Tm ω
E(θ) = ∫(T − Tm) dθΔE = Emax − Emin
½ I (ω² − ω0²) = E(θ)Cs = (ωmax − ωmin)/ω ≈ ΔE/(Iω²)Ineeded = ΔE/(Cs ω²)

The load torque is taken as steady and equal to Tm.

Balancing the reciprocating masses
Force on the frame from cylinder i: Fi = −mrec ai (exact)
primary: mrec r ω² cosθisecondary: (mrec r ω²/n) cos2θi
couple about the middle: M = Σ Fi zizi = (i − (Nc+1)/2) a

With throws at 0°, 180°, 180°, 0° the primary forces and both couples cancel (the crankshaft is mirror-symmetric), but the secondary cranks at 2θi = 0°, 360°, 360°, 0° all point the same way: an unbalanced force of 4 mrec r ω²/n at twice engine speed, which balance shafts turning at 2ω can cancel. With throws at 0°, 180°, 0°, 180° the forces still cancel but the primary couple does not. Gas forces are internal to the engine; they do not shake it up and down but give a rolling reaction −T on the mounts.
